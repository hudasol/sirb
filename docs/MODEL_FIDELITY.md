# Model fidelity: tracker latency and acceleration limit

Generated by `scripts/make_fidelity_doc.py` from `results/fidelity.json` (`scripts/fidelity_study.py`). Do not edit by hand. Simulated results under assumed models; no flight data. Predictions P28 to P30 and their grading are in `docs/PREDICTIONS.md`.

## What was added

Two opt in options in the stress tracker (`StressConfig.latency_s` and `StressConfig.accel_limit`, `sirb.stress.track_limited`). The feedback acceleration is computed from the state `latency_s` earlier and its norm is capped at `accel_limit`; the wind drag acceleration is not capped. With both options off the original linear tracker is used and every earlier result is unchanged. Both values are ASSUMPTIONS: no vehicle datasheet figure for either was retrieved. A state estimator (Kalman style) was not built: it would add noise parameters with no data to set them.

## Delay margin of the assumed loop

For the closed loop with natural frequency 2.0 rad/s and damping 0.9 the gain crossover is 3.75 rad/s, the phase margin 73.5 degrees, and the delay margin 0.342 s (computed by `delay_margin_s` in the study script). A feedback delay above that makes the loop unstable. The explicit discrete implementation behaves as if it had about half a step more delay (step 0.1 s), so a latency of 0.3 s, which is below the continuous margin, already diverges in the simulation; this is consistent with 0.3 s plus half a step exceeding the margin.

## Do the conclusions move?

The GNSS bias and comms loss conclusions do not move for a latency of 0.2 s or for caps of 2 and 4 m/s^2: the violation rates and median separations in the table agree with the baseline. The wind 9 m/s conclusion does move. In the baseline it passes because the loop can cancel the drag; with a cap below the acceleration it needs (table above) the drag cannot be cancelled and the simulated drone drifts. The drag law uses the wind velocity, not the velocity relative to the air, so the size of that drift is not meaningful; the only conclusion is that this pass assumes the vehicle has feedback authority of roughly the table value. Any latency near or above the delay margin makes the result diverge for every case. Stress verdicts in this repository are therefore conditional on adequate control authority and on a feedback delay well below the margin; neither was measured on a vehicle.
